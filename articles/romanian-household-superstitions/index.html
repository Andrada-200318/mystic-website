Romanian Folklore

Romanian Superstitions for Daily Life and Household Luck

Brooms, doors, spoons, bread and fire: the old beliefs said to keep luck, plenty and welcome guests flowing into a Romanian home.

By Andrada Alexiuc · 17 November 2026

✦

The Broom and the Sweeping

The broom rules the house

In Transylvania, the broom is more than a cleaning tool: it is believed to control the flow of luck into the home. That’s why you should never leave two brooms behind the door, or quarrels will move in and luck will stay out.

No sweeping on Easter

In Dolj, people believe that sweeping the house on Easter Day will turn the wheat into rye. The belief comes from the importance of a good harvest and the country’s deep ties to agriculture.

At Christmas, the rubbish stays inside

Sweeping is allowed on Christmas, but the dust and rubbish must not be thrown out of the house, or all your luck will escape through the door along with it.

The Doors

A creaking door lets luck in

Most people see a creaking door as something annoying that needs fixing. In Suceava, however, it is considered a good sign that will bring luck into the house.

Doors, doors, doors…

Romanians clearly put a lot of faith in a few wooden planks. In regions such as Teleorman, Mehedinți, Suceava, and Bucovina, people believe that if a door opens by itself, a visitor is on the way.

The Spoons and the Cutlery

An extra spoon means an extra guest

In Tecuci, if you accidentally set an extra spoon on the table, a hungry guest will soon knock on your door.

Keep the same cutlery for the whole meal

According to folklore, you shouldn’t switch your spoon or fork while eating, or you’ll never feel full. The belief is rooted in the idea of contentment and having enough.

Spoons can disturb your sleep

In Suceava, it is believed that leaving a spoon in the cooking pot overnight can ruin your sleep, causing restless nights or nightmares.

Spoons can even invite poverty

Also in Suceava, people warn against leaving a spoon resting across the rim of a bowl, because poverty might use it to climb into your home.

Licking the spoon and your future husband

In Maramureș, it is said that if an unmarried girl licks her spoon, her future husband will be bald.

At the Table

Spill your coffee, receive money

In counties such as Ilfov, Prahova, Iași, and Argeș, spilling your coffee is a good sign: it means money or some other gain will come your way that day.

Don’t spill pepper on the table

In Suceava, Muntenia, Argeș, and Iași, spilled pepper is believed to predict an argument. The idea comes from linking the sharpness of pepper to the sharp words that might soon be exchanged at the table.

Respect every crumb

Romanians don’t joke about food, and bread in particular is treated as sacred. Bread should never be left on the ground. If it falls, it must be picked up, and leftover crumbs are given to animals instead of being thrown away.

Forgetting the salt brings abundance

If you forget to add salt to the dough when making bread, it is believed to be a sign of future abundance. Your loaf may taste bland, but your future looks bright.

No empty bowls on the table

In Suceava, empty bowls left on the table are a sign of scarcity. Clear them away to make room for abundance.

The Hearth

Respect the fire

In rural Romania, spitting or throwing dirt into the fire is believed to bring bad luck. Fire must be respected, never insulted.
