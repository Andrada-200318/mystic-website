Ophiuchus, the Serpent Bearer: Meet the 13th Zodiac Sign

Why the Sun’s forgotten constellation was left out of the zodiac, and what it means if you were born between November 29 and December 17.

By Andrada Alexiuc · 12 November 2026

✦

Ophiuchus is a constellation found between Scorpius and Sagittarius. It is also known as the Serpent Bearer, because it represents a man holding a serpent. Although the Sun appears to pass through this constellation every year, Ophiuchus was never accepted as one of the twelve traditional zodiac signs.

Why Was It Left Out?

The zodiac system was created by dividing the Sun’s path across the sky into twelve equal parts, one for each month of the year, and then naming each part after a constellation. Including Ophiuchus would have meant dividing the sky into thirteen parts, while the calendar had only twelve months. That wasn’t a practical choice, so Ophiuchus was left out from the beginning.

Why Don’t the Dates Match Anymore?

Because of a slow wobble in Earth’s axis called precession, the spring equinox point, the start of the zodiac, moves backward through the constellations at a rate of about one degree every 72 years. The signs and the constellations share the same names because they once lined up, about 2,000 years ago, but today the difference between the two averages about 24 degrees.

That’s why, according to modern constellation boundaries corrected for precession, people born between November 29 and December 17 were actually born with the Sun in Ophiuchus. In fact, the Sun spends three times as many days in front of Ophiuchus as it does in front of Scorpius, which it crosses in only about a week.

What Does Ophiuchus Mean?

Ophiuchus represents knowledge, healing, and rebirth, as the constellation is a symbolic representation of Asclepius, the Greek god of medicine. Because the snake is associated with wisdom and hidden knowledge, Ophiuchus is often linked to secrets and to discovering what others cannot see.

Its symbol is still with us today. The Rod of Asclepius, a staff with a single snake coiled around it, is named after the Greek god of healing, and it remains a symbol of medicine. It appears in the Star of Life, the emblem of emergency medical services that you can see on ambulances, and in the center of the World Health Organization’s emblem. One explanation is that the snake came to represent healing because it regularly sheds its skin, which makes it seem “reborn.”

Are You an Ophiuchus?

If you were born between November 29 and December 17 and feel drawn to this sign, you are said to share qualities of both Sagittarius and Scorpio. These traits might describe you:

Intelligent and Curious

A natural curiosity runs through your veins. You enjoy solving problems and finding clues, and you have a sharp mind and an eagerness to think things through and form your own judgments.

Charismatic and Influential

People born under Ophiuchus don’t go unnoticed. They know how to capture the attention of a room and are persuasive communicators. They are often drawn to positions of power, which goes hand in hand with their leadership skills.

Independent and Free-Spirited

You want to reclaim the zodiac sign that was taken from you, so it’s clear you value freedom and don’t like being restricted by rules.

Passionate and Determined

You have Scorpio’s intensity, chasing your goals and fighting to achieve them.

You Are a Rare Personality

Since Ophiuchus is not part of the standard Western astrology system, it usually doesn’t appear in astrological calculations. However, if you feel connected to it, you can include it in your personal chart.
