Shengxiao: The Chinese Zodiac

Twelve animals, five elements and a sixty-year cycle: what the year you were born says about you

By Ștefan Murariu · 5 November 2026

✦

The Chinese zodiac, also known as Shengxiao (生肖), is a traditional astrological system based on a repeating cycle of twelve animal signs. Unlike Western astrology, where zodiac signs are determined by the Sun’s position throughout the year, the Chinese zodiac assigns one of twelve animals to each year in a repeating 12-year cycle, following the traditional Chinese calendar.

Shengxiao (生肖) has deep roots in Chinese culture, folklore, and philosophy. Many people believe that the animal sign associated with their birth year can influence different aspects of their lives, from their career paths and relationships to their personalities and overall luck.

The Twelve Chinese Zodiac Signs

鼠

Rat

Shǔ · 1948 · 1960 · 1972 · 1984 · 1996 · 2008 · 2020

The Rat is the first sign of the Chinese zodiac and represents intelligence, adaptability, and resourcefulness.

牛

Ox

Niú · 1949 · 1961 · 1973 · 1985 · 1997 · 2009 · 2021

The Ox symbolizes diligence, reliability, and perseverance.

虎

Tiger

Hǔ · 1950 · 1962 · 1974 · 1986 · 1998 · 2010 · 2022

The Tiger represents courage, confidence, and independence.

兔

Rabbit

Tù · 1951 · 1963 · 1975 · 1987 · 1999 · 2011 · 2023

The Rabbit symbolizes gentleness, compassion, and harmony.

龍

Dragon

Lóng · 1952 · 1964 · 1976 · 1988 · 2000 · 2012 · 2024

The Dragon represents power, ambition, and transformation.

蛇

Snake

Shé · 1953 · 1965 · 1977 · 1989 · 2001 · 2013 · 2025

The Snake symbolizes wisdom, intuition, and depth.

馬

Horse

Mǎ · 1954 · 1966 · 1978 · 1990 · 2002 · 2014 · 2026

The Horse represents freedom, vitality, and movement.

羊

Goat

Yáng · 1955 · 1967 · 1979 · 1991 · 2003 · 2015 · 2027

The Goat (also known as Sheep or Ram) symbolizes compassion, creativity, and emotional sensitivity.

猴

Monkey

Hóu · 1956 · 1968 · 1980 · 1992 · 2004 · 2016 · 2028

The Monkey represents intelligence, adaptability, and curiosity.

雞

Rooster

Jī · 1957 · 1969 · 1981 · 1993 · 2005 · 2017 · 2029

The Rooster symbolizes confidence, honesty, and precision.

狗

Dog

Gǒu · 1958 · 1970 · 1982 · 1994 · 2006 · 2018 · 2030

The Dog represents loyalty, justice, and integrity.

豬

Pig

Zhū · 1959 · 1971 · 1983 · 1995 · 2007 · 2019 · 2031

The Pig symbolizes generosity, abundance, and sincerity.

The Five Elements

In Chinese culture, each year is also associated with one of the five elements: Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, and Water, creating a 60-year cycle. Depending on the element associated with their birth year, each animal sign takes on an additional layer of meaning, further influencing its traditional interpretation. Each element represents a specific type of motion, quality, and life function.

The Five Elements are:

木

Wood

Mù · years ending in 4 or 5

Growth and expansion. Traditionally associated with people who are innovative and curious, optimistic and forward-thinking, compassionate and idealistic, but also motivated by purpose.

火

Fire

Huǒ · years ending in 6 or 7

Transformation and passion. Traditionally associated with people who are expressive and energetic, confident and inspiring, emotionally intense, but also motivated by excitement.

土

Earth

Tǔ · years ending in 8 or 9

Stability and nourishment. Traditionally associated with people who are dependable and calm, emotionally supportive, practical and patient, but also focused on security.

金

Metal

Jīn · years ending in 0 or 1

Structure and clarity. Traditionally associated with people who are organized and focused, honest and principled, analytical and observant, but also very self-disciplined.

水

Water

Shuǐ · years ending in 2 or 3

Flow and wisdom. Traditionally associated with people who are emotionally perceptive, intuitive and thoughtful, calm and adaptable, but also reflective and wise.
